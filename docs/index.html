Maya is a journalist. Noor is her editor. Maya wants to keep her reporting notes private today—but give Noor a way to recover them if she stops checking in. Canary gives her that conditional handoff plan.

JUMP TO A SCENARIO

One concrete story, several possible outcomes

Maya can change the plan.
Noor cannot skip the rules.

Maya encrypts a fictional reporting file for Noor. Her signed check-ins extend the deadline. If she cancels before release, future delivery stops. If she misses a check-in, a grace window gives her another chance.

After that window, keepers need a sufficiently current view of confirmed commands before releasing shares. Noor needs three distinct shares and her own private key to recover the file.

Watch the actions change the result. File encryption, early recovery rejected, deadline extended, cancellation, grace, stale scans, two-share rejection, three-share recovery, wrong-key rejection, and a verified download.

Know which parts are simulated. The browser walkthrough uses real local cryptography with simulated blocks and keeper records. The captured actions are edited for time; no blockchain transactions are sent by this example.

Inspect the native evidence separately. The film also presents a recorded, completed Zcash testnet run with confirmed commands and exact-file recovery. Those receipts are not transactions created by the fictional scenario.

A tested prototype, not an audited tool for sensitive reporting. Use the harmless public fixture. The five native keeper processes currently share one operator and scanner. Three colluding keepers could release early, and delivered shares cannot be recalled. Canary does not determine whether someone is safe, automatically publish a story, or guarantee anonymity. The example explores conditional file recovery for one designated recipient.
